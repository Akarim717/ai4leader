Foundation mini-challenge · Before the six labs

Build Your Personal ChatGPT Workspace

Configure ChatGPT as a working environment rather than a one-off chatbot. Personalise the assistant, organise recurring work into Projects, connect useful plugins, capture reusable AI Skills, and strengthen the prompting habits that make all later labs more effective.

Part A · Personalise ChatGPTHelp the assistant understand how you work
Part B · ChatGPT ProjectsCreate dedicated workspaces for recurring work
Part C · PluginsConnect ChatGPT to useful tools and information
Part E · Reusable AI SkillsTurn proven workflows into repeatable procedures

Learning outcomes

By the end of this mini-challenge, you should be able to personalise ChatGPT, organise work using Projects, identify useful connected tools, create a reusable AI Skill, and use a small set of prompting techniques to improve the reliability of your work.

Part A · Personalise ChatGPT 20 min

Objective. Learn how ChatGPT builds an understanding of you, then improve that understanding without repeating information it already knows.

Step 1 · Check what ChatGPT already knows

Open Settings → Personalisation. Review Enable Memory, Memory Summary, and More about you. Under Memory Summary, select Manage and review the summary.

Ask yourself whether it is accurate, what is outdated, what is missing, and whether it understands your work well enough.

Tip. Your Memory Summary and chat history may already contain much of your background. Avoid unnecessarily repeating the same information in “More about you”.
Step 2 · Identify the gaps

Instead of writing your profile from scratch, ask ChatGPT to discover what it still needs to know.

Help me create a detailed ChatGPT user profile that I can use to personalise my AI assistant. Before writing the profile, first determine what ChatGPT is likely to already know about me from my Memory, previous conversations, and chat history. Do not ask me to repeat information that is probably already known. Instead, ask only about information that would help ChatGPT assist me more effectively but is likely to be missing. Focus on areas such as: - my current professional responsibilities - how I typically use AI - my most frequent workflows - my biggest productivity challenges - the tasks that consume most of my time - the kinds of projects I usually work on - my preferred communication and writing style - how I like information to be presented - the kinds of help I want from ChatGPT - anything else that would make ChatGPT a better long-term thinking partner. Ask one question at a time. Once you have enough information, create a well-written profile suitable for the “More about you” section in ChatGPT Personalisation.
Step 3 · Complete the interview

Think beyond your CV. Useful information includes how you make decisions, how you prefer feedback, recurring tasks you want to automate, outputs you frequently create, your preferred tone and level of detail, and how strongly you want ChatGPT to challenge your ideas.

Step 4 · Update your profile

Copy the final version into More about you. The goal is an assistant that understands not only who you are, but also how you work.

Part B · Organise Your Work with ChatGPT Projects 45 min

Think of a Project as a dedicated digital workspace for a specific area of work. It can bring together related conversations, reference documents, instructions and context so you do not have to rebuild the same background repeatedly.

Step 1 · Discover your AI workflow

Ask ChatGPT to analyse your recurring categories of work and recommend which deserve their own Project.

Review our conversations and identify the major categories of work I regularly ask you to help with. Group similar tasks into logical work areas rather than individual prompts. For each category, explain: - the types of tasks I usually perform - the kinds of documents I frequently create - the reference materials I should upload - whether this category deserves its own ChatGPT Project. Finally, recommend the best set of Projects for organising my future work.
Step 2 · Review the recommendations

Do not accept every suggestion automatically. Ask whether you will return to this work regularly, have many conversations about it, upload reference documents, or benefit from retained context.

Possible university leadership Projects include Strategic Planning, Academic Leadership, Board & Committee Work, Faculty Performance, Policy Development, Workshops & Training, Research, Teaching & Learning, and Personal Productivity.

Step 3 · Create a real Project

Give it a meaningful name, add a short purpose, write Project-specific instructions, and add only appropriate reference documents. Examples include public policies, templates, strategic plans, rubrics, style guides, reports and non-sensitive working documents.

Guided exercise · Build a Project for a recurring workflow

Choose a task you expect to repeat during the next three months. Examples include preparing management papers, monitoring faculty KPIs, developing workshops, reviewing programme proposals, planning meetings, or drafting policy documents.

Act as my ChatGPT Project setup coach. Help me design a focused Project for this recurring workflow: [describe your workflow]. Ask me one question at a time about: - the purpose and intended users - the tasks I repeat most often - the outputs I need - the standards, templates, or frameworks I follow - my preferred writing style and level of detail - the information or files the Project should use - privacy, accuracy, and quality boundaries When you have enough information, produce: 1. a clear Project name 2. a one-sentence purpose 3. concise Project instructions organised under Scope, Working Method, Output Standards, Quality Checks, and Boundaries 4. a list of useful reference files. Do not invent institutional requirements or policies.
Step 4 · Run a context-aware task
Using the instructions and reference materials in this Project, help me complete the following task: [describe one real task]. State any assumptions, identify which Project sources you used, and flag information that is missing. Before finalising, check the output against the quality standards in the Project instructions.

Reflection. Which information belongs in the Project permanently, and which should be supplied only when a specific task requires it?

Part C · Extend ChatGPT with Plugins 30 min

A plugin allows ChatGPT to work with another app, service or source without requiring you to manually move everything back and forth. Depending on permissions, this can reduce copying, searching, tab-switching and repeated setup.

Step 1 · Explore the plugin area

Open Settings → Plugins. Review what is already installed, then browse relevant options such as Google Drive, Gmail, Google Calendar, Google Docs, Google Sheets, Canva, GitHub, data-analysis tools or approved PDF tools. Do not install everything. Choose tools that support a real workflow.

Step 2 · Identify one repetitive workflow

Complete this sentence. I regularly waste time moving between ______ and ______ when I need to ______.

Step 3 · Ask ChatGPT to recommend useful plugins
Based on our conversations and my chat history, analyse how I typically use ChatGPT. Identify the repetitive or multi-step tasks that I perform most frequently. Look for workflows that involve searching, copying information between applications, managing documents, scheduling, writing, analysing files, or creating content. For each workflow: 1. Describe the task. 2. Explain why it is repetitive or time-consuming. 3. Recommend one or more ChatGPT plugins that could simplify the workflow. 4. Explain exactly how each plugin would help. 5. Suggest an improved workflow using the plugin(s). 6. Highlight any privacy, security, or accuracy considerations I should keep in mind. Finally, rank the top five plugins that would have the greatest impact on my productivity and explain why they are the best fit for my work.
Step 4 · Install one relevant plugin

Review what information it can access, whether it can read only or also make changes, whether the workflow involves confidential information, and whether institutional policy permits the connection.

Step 5 · Test a simple, low-risk task
ToolExample task
Google DriveFind documents related to a strategic initiative and summarise what each contains.
GmailFind recent emails about an upcoming meeting and summarise the actions required.

Step 6 · Compare old and new workflows

What manual steps disappeared? How many app switches were avoided? What still needs human checking? Did the plugin improve quality, speed, or both?

Responsible-use principle. Start with low-risk information, use the minimum permissions needed, verify outputs, and keep consequential actions under human control.
Part E · Create Reusable AI Skills 60 min

Many important tasks are not single prompts. They are repeatable processes. Think of an AI Skill as a standard operating procedure that the AI follows whenever it performs a particular task.

PromptBest for one-off requests or simple tasks.
Custom assistantBest for a specialist role with persistent instructions and knowledge.
SkillBest for a repeatable multi-step workflow with defined steps and checks.

A useful analogy is that a prompt asks an experienced colleague for help, while a Skill gives that colleague a proven workflow to follow.

Step 1 · Identify a workflow suitable for a Skill
Based on everything you know about my work, identify five recurring workflows that involve multiple steps and would benefit from being converted into reusable AI Skills. For each workflow, explain: - why it is suitable - the typical sequence of steps - the expected inputs - the expected outputs - who would benefit from using it.
Step 2 · Choose one workflow

Examples for university leaders include preparing a Board paper, reviewing a programme proposal, analysing a strategic initiative, designing a workshop, evaluating a policy draft, preparing a management briefing, or conducting a benchmarking study.

Step 3 · Design the Skill
Help me design a reusable AI Skill for the following workflow. Workflow: [Describe the workflow.] Produce the Skill in the following format: - Skill name - Purpose - Target users - Required inputs - Expected outputs - Step-by-step workflow - Decision points - Questions the AI should ask when information is missing - Quality checks before producing the final output - Common mistakes to avoid - Suggestions for improving the output
Step 4 · Test and deliberately try to break it

Run the Skill with a realistic case, then run a weak or incomplete case. A trustworthy Skill should identify missing information rather than quietly invent it.

Step 5 · Refine and version it

Ask the AI which instructions were ambiguous, skipped or insufficient, revise only what is needed, and save the improved version.

Facilitator's key message. A good Skill does not make AI more intelligent. It makes AI more consistent. The productivity gain comes from capturing your best way of working and turning it into a reusable workflow.
Level Up Your Prompting Skill Practice

Small additions to a prompt can prevent misunderstandings and produce stronger outputs. Try these techniques with a real leadership task.

1 · Ask for clarifying questions first
Before starting the task, review my request and identify any missing or ambiguous information that could materially affect the result. Ask only the essential clarifying questions, one at a time, and wait for my answers. When you have enough information, briefly confirm the agreed goal, audience, constraints, deliverable, and success criteria before proceeding. If nothing important is missing, say so and begin the task.
2 · Ask AI to confirm the task
Before doing anything, show me exactly what you understood from my request, including the goal, constraints, and what you are going to change.
3 · Ask AI to determine the approach first
First, determine the best approach based on my goal and constraints. Then create the final version.
4 · Give AI a role and a clear goal
Act as an expert [role]. Your goal is to [clear objective]. Review [the material or task] and identify [what matters most for a successful result].
5 · Tell AI what not to do
Do not use corporate language, unnecessary headings, generic advice, unsupported claims, or short, choppy sentences.
6 · Ask AI to critique and improve its own answer
Now critique your own answer. Identify the weakest parts, explain what could be improved, then rewrite it into a stronger final version.

Practice. Choose one prompt you used earlier. Add at least two techniques above, run it again, and compare the two outputs. Which addition changed the result most, and what misunderstanding did it prevent?

Challenge 1 · Key takeaway

ChatGPT becomes much more useful when you treat it as an AI workspace rather than a place for isolated questions. Personalisation introduces the assistant to your way of working. Projects organise recurring work. Plugins provide access to useful tools and information. Skills capture proven workflows. Better prompting improves the quality of all four.

Activity 1 · Build Your Own Executive Kanban AppApprox. 35 minutes · From prompting to a working productivity tool

The challenge

Use ChatGPT to create a simple, usable Kanban Board web app for your own leadership work. Do not just ask for a mock-up. The app should actually let you enter, move, review and summarise tasks.

Minimum viable app

  • Inbox / New for captured tasks
  • In Progress for active work
  • Waiting / KIV for dependencies
  • Review / Revise for work requiring refinement
  • Done for completed work

I prefer “Waiting / KIV” to “Pending” because it makes the reason for inactivity more explicit.

Key features

  • Quick new-task entry
  • Title, description, owner and due date
  • Colour-coded urgency labels such as Critical, High, Normal and Low
  • Drag-and-drop or simple Move controls
  • Overdue and due-soon alerts
  • Search and filter
  • Weekly and monthly summaries showing completed, overdue, carried-forward and next actions
  • Local browser storage so tasks persist

Facilitator cue

After participants get a basic app working, ask them to improve one feature rather than starting over. The learning point is iterative specification. A good executive assistant improves through dialogue.

I want you to build a polished single-page Kanban Board web app for a university leader. The purpose is to manage executive and academic-management tasks clearly and quickly. Build the app as a self-contained HTML/CSS/JavaScript application that can run in a browser without a server or external database. KANBAN COLUMNS 1. Inbox / New 2. In Progress 3. Waiting / KIV 4. Review / Revise 5. Done CORE TASK FIELDS - Task title - Short description / notes - Owner or person responsible - Date created - Due date - Urgency label: Critical, High, Normal, Low - Optional category such as Strategy, Academic, People, Finance, Student, Governance, External ESSENTIAL FEATURES - Prominent “New Task” input or button. - Add, edit, duplicate and delete tasks. - Move tasks between columns using drag-and-drop if practical, with a reliable Move control as fallback. - Colour-coded urgency labels. Use restrained professional colours and do not rely on colour alone; always show the urgency text. - Highlight overdue tasks clearly and show a “Due soon” alert for tasks due within 3 days. - Search tasks by keyword. - Filter by urgency, category, owner and due status. - Show counts on each Kanban column. - Persist all data in localStorage so the board remains after browser refresh. - Add a small dashboard showing Open, In Progress, Waiting, Overdue and Completed This Month. - Add Weekly Summary and Monthly Summary buttons. Each summary should show: • tasks completed, • tasks still open, • overdue tasks, • tasks carried forward, • important upcoming deadlines, • suggested further actions. - Allow the summary to be copied to the clipboard as clean text for an email or management update. - Include sample tasks on first launch so I can immediately see how the app works, plus a button to clear sample data. - Make the interface responsive for laptop and mobile use. DESIGN Use a modern, minimalist executive dashboard style. Clean sans-serif typography, generous spacing, subtle borders, accessible contrast, restrained colour and no decorative clutter. The board should feel suitable for a Vice Chancellor, Dean or Head of Department. Activity 2 · From Deep Research to a Board-Ready Pitching DeckApprox. 45 minutes · Research → judgement → storyline → consulting-style presentation

Board scenario

You are the university's top management team. You are seeking Board approval for a RM15 million, three-year AI-Enabled Lifelong Learning & Digital Campus Initiative.

The initiative aims to diversify revenue beyond traditional degree enrolment, build stackable adult-learning and microcredential pathways, improve digital student services and introduce responsible AI-enabled productivity across selected university operations.

The Board will ask a simple question: Why should we invest now, and why is this the right strategic bet?

Your task

Design primer

Before creating slides, explore the Deckary guide and capture the principles you want ChatGPT to follow. Focus on how consulting decks use message-led slide titles, storyline, evidence hierarchy, page structure, charts and visual discipline.

Open Deckary guide ↗

Do not merely imitate appearance. Ask what makes the deck easy for a Board member to understand and act on.

Learning output

8–10 slide Board Pitch Deck

  • Executive recommendation
  • Why now
  • External evidence and opportunity
  • Strategic fit
  • Proposed initiative
  • Economics / value case
  • Risks and mitigations
  • Implementation roadmap
  • Decision required from the Board
Conduct deep research to help a university top-management team decide whether to propose a RM15 million, three-year AI-Enabled Lifelong Learning & Digital Campus Initiative to its Board. The initiative has four intended components: 1. stackable lifelong-learning, professional and microcredential offerings for adult learners, 2. digital recruitment, onboarding and learner-support services, 3. responsible AI-enabled productivity in selected university administrative and academic-support workflows, 4. the digital capability, governance and staff development needed to sustain the change. Research the case from a Malaysian higher-education perspective, while using international evidence where it provides useful benchmarks. Investigate: - shifts in university demographics and traditional student demand, - adult learning and lifelong-learning demand, - employer demand for shorter and stackable learning, - the microcredential and online-learning landscape, - relevant Malaysian policy or regulatory considerations, - examples of comparable universities or education providers, - realistic revenue and cost drivers, - digital capability and organisational prerequisites, - AI governance, privacy, cybersecurity and reputational risks, - likely implementation barriers, - evidence on what commonly causes digital-transformation or lifelong-learning initiatives to underperform. Use current, credible sources. Prefer primary sources, regulators, government publications, institutional reports and reputable research. Cite every material factual claim. Separate verified evidence from inference and assumptions. Do not begin by trying to justify the initiative. Test whether the proposal is strategically defensible. Identify evidence that weakens the case as well as evidence that supports it. Finish with: A. an executive synthesis of the 5–7 findings that matter most to a university Board, B. a balanced case FOR and AGAINST the investment, C. the 5 biggest assumptions that must be validated, D. a recommended decision and rationale, E. a source table with links, publication dates and what each source supports.
I now have a deep-research report for a Board proposal. I want you to help me turn it into a professional consulting-style presentation. Before designing the deck, read and analyse this guide: https://deckary.com/blog/pillar-consulting-presentations-guide Step 1 — Extract the design principles Summarise the most decision-relevant presentation principles from the webpage. Translate them into a practical design checklist for this deck. Pay particular attention to storyline, takeaway/action titles, information hierarchy, charts, page architecture, evidence, whitespace, consistency and executive readability. If you cannot access the webpage, tell me clearly and ask me to paste the relevant content rather than pretending you have read it. Step 2 — Analyse my research Use the deep-research output I provide below. Identify the 5–7 insights that matter most to the Board. Do not include facts simply because they are interesting. Every slide must advance the Board's decision. Step 3 — Build the storyline Create a tight SCQA-style or answer-first narrative suitable for a Board discussion. The recommended decision should be clear early. Make the logic flow from context → evidence → strategic choice → value → risk → execution → decision required. Step 4 — Design an 8–10 slide deck For every slide provide: - slide number, - an action / takeaway title written as a complete message, not a topic label, - the single purpose of the slide, - recommended visual structure, - exact content or concise copy, - chart or diagram recommendation where useful, - evidence/source note, - speaker note with the point I should make orally. Avoid generic decorative imagery. Prefer charts, matrices, timelines, simple diagrams, quantified callouts and clean tables when they improve the argument. Do not fabricate numbers. If evidence is missing, show the assumption explicitly. The scenario: Our top-management team is seeking Board approval for a RM15 million, three-year AI-Enabled Lifelong Learning & Digital Campus Initiative. The Board needs to decide whether to approve the investment, reject it, or approve it conditionally with stage gates. Required closing slide: “Decision requested from the Board” with the amount requested, what it funds, proposed stage gates, success measures and the immediate next action. Visual direction: Professional strategy-consulting style, minimalist, restrained corporate palette, strong grid, generous whitespace, high information density without clutter, no gimmicky transitions, and consistent typography. One idea per slide. Make the deck feel Board-ready rather than like a training presentation. After the outline is approved, create the presentation deck using the available presentation-generation capability. Then perform a final quality check for logical flow, unsupported claims, inconsistent numbers, unreadable charts, weak slide titles and visual clutter. AI as Your Institutional Intelligence Analyst

Research a strategic question without confusing plausible answers with evidence.

⌄

Your challenge

Should our university expand its ODL portfolio? Frame what management needs to know before making the decision.

Learning output

Institutional Intelligence Brief

  • Decision question
  • Evidence table
  • Opportunities and risks
  • Knowledge gaps
  • Management recommendation

Suggested tools

ChatGPT Deep Research, Gemini Deep Research, Perplexity and NotebookLM where available.

Facilitator twist

Seed one impressive but unsupported market-demand statistic. Reward teams that reject or qualify it rather than repeat it.

Help me prepare an institutional intelligence brief on whether a university should expand its online and distance learning portfolio. Structure the analysis around market demand, learner segments, competitors, regulation, internal capability, financial sustainability and strategic fit. For every significant claim, distinguish between verified evidence, inference and assumption. Flag missing evidence. Do not turn uncertainty into certainty. End with three management options and the evidence still needed before a final decision.

AI as Your Data Analyst

Move from spreadsheet to management judgement.

⌄

Your challenge

The VC wants to know why enrolment is below target, which programmes require attention and what management should investigate next.

Download the synthetic university dataset (.xlsx)

Fictional Universiti Cakrawala data, 2023–2026. The workbook includes programme performance, management targets, data definitions and a quick-view sheet. It also contains a few issues that careful analysts should detect.

Learning output

Performance Insight Brief

  • Key trends
  • Anomalies
  • One chart
  • Five management questions
  • Evidence-based next steps

Suggested tools

ChatGPT Data Analysis, Excel, Gemini or another tool that can inspect spreadsheet data.

Facilitator twist

Include an apparent decline caused by a change in reporting definition or a broken formula. Ask whether the dashboard is telling the truth.

Act as a university management data analyst. I have uploaded the Universiti Cakrawala synthetic management workbook. Inspect the workbook before drawing conclusions. Read the README, Management_Targets and Data_Notes sheets before analysing Programme_Data. 1. Describe the workbook structure, variables, missing values, duplicates, implausible values and any other data-quality concerns. 2. Analyse enrolment target attainment from 2023–2026 by faculty and programme. Identify which trends are improving, stable or deteriorating. 3. Examine retention, student satisfaction, graduate employment, contribution margin, staffing ratio and the applications-to-acceptance funnel. 4. Identify the 5–7 most decision-relevant findings and calculate the evidence supporting each finding. 5. Flag any apparent trend that may be affected by a reporting-definition change or data-quality issue. Do not silently repair questionable data. 6. Create one or two management visualisations that reveal the most important patterns rather than simply displaying all available metrics. 7. Separate clearly what the data shows, what might explain it, and what additional evidence would be needed to test those explanations. 8. End with five questions the Vice Chancellor and management team should investigate next and three evidence-based management actions that are justified now. Do not infer causation unless the data supports it. Where the dataset is unreliable, say so explicitly.

AI as Your Strategic Thinking Partner

⌄

How to use this activity bank

These six activities are alternatives rather than six compulsory exercises. For a 45-minute session, the facilitator should select one substantial activity or two shorter ones. The remaining activities can be used later as reusable leadership practices.

Activity 1 · The Red Team ChallengeStress-test UNITEN’s proposed 2027–2029 strategic framework before leadership adopts it

The challenge

Your team is advising UNITEN leadership on the Proposed Strategic Framework for UNITEN, 2027 to 2029. The framework connects teaching, research, campus operations and industry around the energy transition, SDG 7, ESG and Education for Sustainable Development. Your job is not to promote it. Your job is to find where it is strong, where it is vulnerable, what evidence is still missing, and what must be revised before senior leadership or the Board should endorse it.

Download / open the proposal↗

Learning output

Strategic Vulnerability Map + Endorsement Advice

  • Strategic assumption
  • Red-team challenge
  • Evidence available
  • Evidence still required
  • Revision proposed
  • Endorse, endorse with conditions, or do not endorse

What to stress-test

  • Whether the five pillars form a coherent strategy rather than a collection of initiatives
  • Whether UNITEN can credibly claim leadership as an “energy university”
  • Whether the 2027–2029 targets are evidence-based and achievable
  • Whether governance and ownership are strong enough to prevent the framework becoming another plan on the shelf
  • Whether the resourcing model is credible without a defined ringgit envelope
  • Whether the relationship with TNB creates both advantage and independence risk
  • Whether the Energy Core, microcredentials, Challenge Bank, Living Lab and Energy Clinics can be executed at scale
  • Whether rankings and SDG 7 positioning are outcomes of strategy or are quietly driving it

Facilitator cue

Do not ask teams whether they “like” the framework. Ask what they would change before putting their own name behind it. Push them to separate strategic weakness, evidence weakness and execution weakness.

Act as a rigorous Red Team reviewing UNITEN's “Proposed Strategic Framework for UNITEN, 2027 to 2029”. The framework aims to connect teaching, research, campus operations and industry around the energy transition, SDG 7, ESG and Education for Sustainable Development. It proposes five pillars: 1. Education for Sustainable Development 2. Lifelong Learning and Workforce Transition 3. Research with a Destination 4. Campus as a Living Lab and ESG in Practice 5. Reach, Access and Regional Role It also proposes major initiatives such as the Energy Core, Energy Transition Pathways, a Challenge Bank, a Living Lab and Energy Clinics, supported by a new governance structure, scorecard and 2027–2029 roadmap. Do not try to be supportive. Be critical but fair. Your role is to help us decide whether the framework should be endorsed, revised or rejected. Challenge us one issue at a time. Examine: - strategic coherence and distinctiveness - fit with UNITEN's identity as an energy university - alignment with SDG 7, NETR, RMK13 and the Higher Education Blueprint - strength of evidence behind the proposed targets - feasibility of implementation from 2027 to 2029 - governance, ownership and accountability - staff capability and initiative overload - resourcing and financial sustainability - dependence on TNB and possible research-independence concerns - student, industry and community value - risks of greenwashing or rankings-driven behaviour - opportunity costs and what UNITEN would have to stop doing - unintended consequences For each challenge, ask us to respond before you move to the next issue. Do not accept vague answers. Ask for evidence, assumptions, trade-offs and ownership. At the end, produce: 1. A Strategic Vulnerability Map with columns for assumption, challenge, evidence available, evidence still required, and revision needed. 2. The three strongest elements of the framework. 3. The three most serious weaknesses. 4. Five revisions that would materially strengthen the proposal. 5. A recommendation: ENDORSE, ENDORSE WITH CONDITIONS, or DO NOT ENDORSE, with reasons.
Activity 2 · The Strategic Fork in the RoadMake a choice under uncertainty, then revisit it when the world changes

The scenario

The university has RM20 million for strategic investment over three years. It cannot fully fund everything. Choose among ODL and lifelong learning, digital infrastructure, research capability, or student experience and employability.

Learning output

Before-and-After Strategic Decision Brief

  • Original decision
  • Decision criteria
  • New information
  • What changed
  • Final recommendation

Possible strategic shocks

  • Budget reduced by 30%
  • Enrolment falls another 12%
  • A competitor launches a low-cost online university
  • New regulatory requirement
  • Major industry partner withdraws

Facilitator cue

Do not tell teams the shock in advance. Reveal it after they have publicly committed to a decision.

I will then provide new information that changes the context. After the new information, help us decide whether the original decision should be retained, modified or abandoned. Make us explain what evidence or assumption changed.
Activity 3 · The Board of Difficult VoicesDefend a strategic proposal against legitimate but competing stakeholder concerns

The challenge

Your university proposes aggressive adoption of generative AI across teaching, assessment and administration. AI will sequentially play the Chair of the Board, CFO, Academic Senate representative, student representative, CIO and employer representative.

Learning output

Board-Ready Recommendation

  • Final recommendation
  • Stakeholder concerns
  • Revisions made
  • Unresolved tensions
  • Leadership rationale

What this develops

Perspective taking, stakeholder intelligence, trade-off management and the ability to distinguish disagreement from poor reasoning.

Facilitator cue

Ask whether the proposal became more cautious, more ambitious or simply more defensible after the stakeholder dialogue.

Sample proposal for immediate use

Proposal title: UNITEN AI-Enabled University Transformation Programme 2027–2029

UNITEN proposes a three-year university-wide programme to embed generative AI across teaching, assessment, research support and administrative operations. The programme seeks Board approval for an initial investment of RM12 million over three years.

The proposal has five components:

  • Teaching and learning: provide approved AI tools for staff and students, redesign selected courses for AI-enabled learning, and develop staff capability in AI-integrated pedagogy.
  • Assessment: redesign high-risk assessments so that students must demonstrate reasoning, judgement, evidence use and process, rather than simply submit polished outputs.
  • Research and innovation: provide secure AI assistants for literature synthesis, coding, data analysis, grant development and research administration.
  • Administration: automate selected repetitive processes such as meeting summaries, report preparation, FAQ handling, document drafting and management dashboards.
  • Governance and infrastructure: establish an institutional AI policy, secure enterprise platforms, staff training, data governance, cybersecurity safeguards and an AI governance committee.

Expected benefits: lower administrative workload, faster turnaround time, improved student support, stronger digital capability, more consistent use of AI across the university, and better preparation of graduates for AI-enabled workplaces.

Key concerns already raised: recurring licence costs after Year 3, possible academic integrity problems, unequal AI capability among staff, privacy and data-security risks, overdependence on commercial vendors, uncertainty about measurable productivity gains, and whether the university has sufficient change-management capacity.

Decision requested from the Board: approve the RM12 million envelope and authorise a phased implementation beginning with six pilot faculties and five administrative functions, subject to annual review against agreed educational, financial, operational and risk indicators.

The build challenge

Use ChatGPT to create a responsive web app called Board Challenger. The app acts as a critical but constructive Board member and uses Socratic dialogue rather than immediately giving answers.

Learning output

Board Challenger web app

  • Brainstorm mode
  • Stress-test mode
  • Board-pitch rehearsal
  • Project review
  • Difficult-question rehearsal
  • Board-readiness summary

Core behaviour

The app should probe strategic alignment, evidence, financial viability, stakeholder impact, implementation capability, governance, risks, sustainability and measurable outcomes.

Facilitator cue

Ask participants to compare the first version of their idea with the version after 6–10 Socratic exchanges. Did the app improve the proposal or merely increase confidence?

Create a responsive single-page web app called “Board Challenger” for senior university leaders. The app acts as a critical but constructive university Board member using a Socratic dialogue approach. The user should be able to choose one of these modes: - Brainstorm an idea - Stress-test a strategic proposal - Prepare for a Board pitch - Review a project proposal - Rehearse difficult Board questions The AI Board member must not immediately give answers or recommendations. It should ask one probing question at a time. Its questions should examine: - strategic alignment - evidence and assumptions - financial viability - stakeholder impact - implementation capability - risks and unintended consequences - governance - sustainability - measurable outcomes After each user response, briefly identify what is strong, what remains unclear and what assumption needs further examination. After 6 to 10 exchanges, generate a summary containing: - strongest aspects of the proposal - weak assumptions - unanswered questions - evidence still required - likely Board objections - recommended improvements - readiness rating from 1 to 5 Include a proposal text box, mode selection, conversation area, progress indicator, reset button, copy-summary button and export-to-text function. Use a clean executive design suitable for university senior management. Build it in HTML, CSS and JavaScript in a single file. Store the conversation locally in the browser. If live AI integration is unavailable, build the interface and clearly mark where the API call should be connected.

The challenge

Benchmark UNITEN against selected Malaysian, regional and international universities. Do not reduce the exercise to ranking positions. Define what genuine leadership in SDG 7 should mean, then compare evidence across institutions.

Learning output

Strategic Benchmarking Dashboard

  • UNITEN position
  • Malaysian peers
  • Regional peers
  • International peers
  • Gap or opportunity
  • Three strategic moves

Suggested dimensions

Energy-related programmes, energy transition research, industry partnerships, campus energy management, innovation centres, talent development, lifelong learning, policy contribution, commercialisation and living-lab activity.

Facilitator cue

Push teams beyond imitation. Ask, “What should UNITEN do differently rather than simply better?”

Conduct a strategic benchmarking study of Universiti Tenaga Nasional against selected Malaysian, ASEAN and international universities in relation to SDG 7, Affordable and Clean Energy. Do not rely on rankings alone. First propose a defensible set of benchmarking dimensions across research, education, industry collaboration, campus energy practice, innovation, policy contribution, talent development, lifelong learning and public engagement. Explain why each dimension matters. Then recommend a balanced set of peer institutions and explain why each is a useful comparator. Use verifiable sources and cite every material claim. Separate clearly: - documented evidence - reasonable interpretation - missing or uncertain information Identify: - areas where UNITEN appears distinctive - areas where peers are stronger - strategic gaps - practices worth adapting - areas where copying competitors would not make strategic sense Finish with three strategic moves that could materially strengthen UNITEN’s SDG 7 positioning and five questions senior management should investigate next.
Activity 6 · Foresight LabUNITEN 2040, becoming a true Energy University

The foresight question

What would UNITEN need to become by 2040 to be recognised nationally and internationally as a true Energy University? Explore multiple plausible futures rather than trying to predict one future.

Learning output

UNITEN Energy University 2040 Strategic Foresight Map

  • Driving forces
  • Critical uncertainties
  • Four scenarios
  • Strategic implications
  • Robust strategies
  • Backcast actions

Possible driving forces

Energy transition, renewables, storage, hydrogen, AI and smart grids, nuclear, climate regulation, carbon markets, electrification, energy security, geopolitics, workforce shifts, lifelong learning and industry-funded research.

Facilitator cue

Use the question, “If this future occurred, what would UNITEN wish it had started doing in 2026?” It usually produces more useful thinking than asking what UNITEN should do today.

Act as a strategic foresight partner for Universiti Tenaga Nasional. Our ambition is to explore what UNITEN must become by 2040 to be recognised nationally and internationally as a true Energy University. Do not predict one future. Guide us through the exercise step by step. Help us: 1. define what “true Energy University” should mean in 2040, 2. identify major driving forces, weak signals and disruptions affecting energy, higher education, research, talent and industry, 3. rank the drivers by impact and uncertainty, 4. choose two critical uncertainties, 5. construct four distinct but plausible future scenarios, 6. analyse implications, threats, opportunities and capabilities required in each scenario, 7. identify strategies that remain valuable across multiple scenarios, 8. backcast from 2040 to 2035, 2030, 2027 and the next 12 months. Challenge our assumptions throughout. Do not simply agree with our preferred future. At the end, produce a one-page Strategic Foresight Map using this structure: Drivers → Scenarios → Strategic implications → Robust strategies → Backcast actions.

Reject any design that automatically sends sensitive information or makes consequential decisions without human review.
